About Virtru: While the rest of the security industry obsesses over locking data down to prevent it from being lost or stolen, we're doing something fundamentally different at Virtru. We're setting data free so that you can intentionally share it with others, but without sacrificing security, privacy, or control. We've created both a suite of powerful data protection applications and an open platform that's sparking an ecosystem of innovation. Through the Trusted Data Format (TDF) open standard, we're not just protecting data; we're creating a new paradigm where security enables sharing rather than preventing it. Think of us as the Android of data protection: a robust platform with an open core that developers and partners can build upon, coupled with our own best-in-class applications that showcase what's possible when you reimagine security from the ground up. Backed by Iconiq Capital, Bessemer Venture Partners, Foundry Capital, and Tiger Global, we're helping Fortune 500 companies and government agencies discover that true data security means having the freedom to share, collaborate, and innovate — without compromise. Compensation: $200,000-225,000/year Team & Position Details: Virtru protects data wherever it travels, for customers ranging from commercial enterprises to the Department of Defense, the Intelligence Community, Law Enforcement, and Federal Civilian agencies. Many of these customers run Virtru inside classified mission environments across the Washington, DC region, where the work happens on site next to the customer's own engineers, and where what we ship has to fit systems few people outside the building can see. As a Staff Forward Deployed Engineer in the Delivery Engineering organization, you'd be helping to build a founder team focused on making these deployments simple, easy, and fast. You'll be the senior engineer who can change the software in the room. You'll design and build the integrations, policy enforcement points (the components that enforce access policy inside a customer's application), and deployment tooling our customers need and cannot get from the product alone, and you'll own the technical outcome of each deployment from zero to one and on into production. You'll work across several cleared customers in the DC region, owning each deployment you're staffed to and turning the problem several customers share into one reusable fix. You will work with deployment engineers who run installs and upgrades through our published paths, with solutions architects who shaped each engagement, with QA to prove what you build before it ships, and with our product engineering and Platform Engineering counterparts, who need what you learn in the field as designs and decisions they can act on. You'll also help set the bar for the forward deployed engineers who join after you. The impact of this role is company and product wide, heavily enabling our goals to scale Virtru, partner, and customer led deployments non-linearly without having to throw more humans at the problem. You'll be expected to come up with AI-native design patterns that will increase our velocity without compromising our high bar for quality and security. The right candidate is excited about working on a small, nimble, highly autonomous and empowered team where everyone is expected to make high judgement, high velocity decisions independently and with urgency. If you want a role where the connection between what you build and what the customer achieves requires no explaining, this is one. As a Staff Forward Deployed Engineer, your responsibilities will include: • Design and ship software inside cleared customer environments. Integrations, policy enforcement points, connectors, and deployment tooling, written in Go, Python, Java, or Rust, reviewed, tested with QA, and built so the next customer can reuse them. • Own the technical outcome of each deployment you're staffed to. From the first environment review through production and upgrade, you are the engineer accountable for the platform working in the customer's systems, across Kubernetes, Windows estates, identity providers, and air-gapped classified networks. • Turn field lessons into product decisions. Write designs and architecture decision records that weigh a product fix against a field workaround, take them to our product engineering teams with evidence, and see them through to a decision. • Resolve conflicts between a customer's accreditation boundary and the platform's requirements by changing the design, and write the pattern down so the next engagement inherits it. • Diagnose failures down to source code, whether the cause sits in our software, in the customer's environment, or in the space between them. • Build validated configurations for identity providers beyond our reference setup, such as PingFederate, Entra ID, and Okta, so the next customer configures what you built instead of starting over. • Work inside codebases other teams own, and leave them better tested and documented than you found them. • Package what you build so it installs, upgrades, and runs in disconnected environments under STIG hardening and FIPS constraints. • Partner with deployment engineers so standard installs follow the published path, and hand them what you build with documentation, test evidence, and a clear way to report back. • Explain your designs to customer security reviewers and accrediting officials, and answer their follow-up questions. • Mentor other forward deployed engineers, review their code and designs, and help define how we interview and level for the role. • Use AI coding agents as part of your daily workflow, and help the team learn which patterns raise quality and speed together. • This role requires residence in the DMV and on-site work at customer locations at least 50% of the time, and may require travel to other customer sites in the DC region. Skills that will help you thrive in this role: • Must haves • An active TS/SCI US security clearance. This role will work in classified environments at least 50% of the time. • You ship production code today, and other people reuse what you design. You write and review code in Go, Python, Java, or Rust as part of your current job, and you can walk us through a component you designed that another team or customer adopted. • You have made software work in environments you did not build. You have diagnosed identity, certificate, or Kubernetes failures in a classified, accredited, or customer production environment from logs, without the original author on the call, and changed a design to fit the environment rather than asking for an exception. • Nice to haves • Kubernetes across more than one distribution, such as OpenShift, Rancher, or RKE2, including Helm packaging. • Delivering software into air-gapped environments: offline registries, bundle transfer, and upgrades with no network. • Identity and authorization integration: OIDC, SAML, PingFederate, Entra ID, Okta, CAC or PIV, and attribute-based access control (ABAC). • Windows and .NET depth: Windows Services, signed installers, Active Directory, and Group Policy. • DISA STIGs, FIPS 140, and the Risk Management Framework from the delivery side. • Familiarity with Zero Trust security architecture and concepts such as Policy Enforcement and Decision Points, cryptography, and identity. • Design documents or decision records another team adopted. • Experience building with AI coding agents. • Prior forward deployed, embedded, or customer-site engineering work with Intelligence Community or Department of Defense customers. • Where this role may not be a fit • Having stepped away from writing code. If your recent work is advising, configuring, or reviewing rather than writing and shipping code yourself, this role may not be for you. • Wanting a product-only role with no customer contact. Much of this job is working next to customers, in their environments, on their problems. If you prefer to build away from customers, this role may not be for you. • Inability to earn trust and credibility with partners and customers. You may be the person who has to explain to a skeptical government security team why our software is safe, or to an internal VP of Engineering why we need to prioritize critical platform features. Those have to be conversations you are willing and excited to have.
