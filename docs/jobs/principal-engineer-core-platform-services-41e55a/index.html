About the Core Platform Services Team Target’s Infrastructure strategy is built around one idea: make the right thing the easy thing. The Target Application Platform (TAP) is where that happens. Builders tell TAP what they need, and the platform, more and more with help from AI agents, gets it done safely. In an agentic world, many more people create software. Builders at Target include engineers, product managers, UX designers, data scientists, and analysts. Core Platform Services builds the common services that connect Target’s platforms, so builders get a delightful experience that feels like one connected platform. We work closely with our Product Manager to find where builders lose the most time and decide what to build next. Our first major capability is Action Center, along with the tools that help builders act on it: • Action Center. A shared service and API that platform teams use to publish the work they need from builders, such as instrumenting with OpenTelemetry or upgrading a runtime. It tracks that work across the more than 5,000 applications on TAP and gives each one a single prioritized list, in place of requests scattered across tickets, chat, and email. • Remediation guides. A shared framework that lets a platform team write the fix for a common problem once and make it available to every application. • Agentic remediation. AI agents that follow a guide, make the code change, and open a pull request for the builder to review, either in the background on TAP or inside the builder’s own coding agent. A person approves every change. Action Center is a starting point. Over time, the team will build the features and integrations that remove friction across the full builder experience, from starting a new project to running it in production. We track adoption, time to complete, and manual work removed, and use that data to decide what comes next. We serve two groups: the builders who create software on TAP, and the platform teams who support them. We earn adoption by building services people want to use, and we measure success in builder time returned. The platform has already given back more than 500,000 hours, and this team’s work is a big part of growing that number. We operate business-critical services in 24x7 production environments. Technologies commonly used across the team include Golang, Kubernetes, Kafka, React, the Model Context Protocol (MCP), and agentic AI patterns. About the Role As a Principal Engineer, you are the senior-most individual-contributor technical leader on the Core Platform Services team. You partner with the Senior Engineering Manager and the team’s Product Manager to set the technical vision, architecture, and standards for the team, and to remove friction across the full builder experience. This is a hands-on role: you lead through depth, design, and influence rather than through direct reports. Principal Engineers operate at the overall Infrastructure level. While you are assigned to Core Platform Services, you are expected to contribute meaningfully to broader Infrastructure priorities, including problems that lie outside your team. You will be accountable for: • Technical direction. Owning the end-to-end architecture of Core Platform Services as it grows, from Action Center and agentic remediation today to the features and integrations that come next, along with the APIs and MCP servers that other platform teams and AI agents build on. • The hardest problems. Designing multi-tenant systems that many independent teams can extend without breaking each other, and AI agents that can safely change code for builders. That means least-privilege access, a clear record of every change, and human review. • Platform as a product. Partnering with the Product Manager to treat builder experience as an internal product, with self-service by default, adoption you can measure, and services that work for builders with or without a deep engineering background. • Engineering standards. Setting technical standards and leading design and architecture reviews across the team and partner platform teams. • Infrastructure impact. Contributing to Infrastructure-wide technical priorities beyond Core Platform Services. • Enterprise influence. Building relationships with Principal Engineers across Target to learn where builders hit the most friction and where the biggest opportunities are, and working with the Product Manager to turn what you learn into the roadmap. You also represent Core Platform Services in enterprise architecture forums. Success in this role requires exceptional technical judgment, clear communication, operational discipline, and the ability to drive clarity and consensus in ambiguous, high-scale environments.
