Why join us? Mitti by SafetyCulture is a global tech company, just not the kind you're picturing. Our almost 1,000-person team builds tools that make work better for the three billion frontline workers who keep the world moving. We've got big tech scale, without the sign-off layers or corporate theatre. Every permanent team member gets equity, so when we grow, you do too. This next chapter is about scaling smart, powered by AI, a clear vision and real ownership. Big tech impact, without the big tech ick. As a Software Engineer II in the Customer Identity and Access Management (CIAM) team, you deliver well-scoped services and features inside an architecture our Senior and Staff engineers set, and grow real depth in the identity domain as we modernise the platform. You will work with a modern tech stack including Golang on the backend, identity and access technologies such as OpenID Connect and Security Assertion Markup Language (SAML), Amazon Web Services (AWS) , Auth0 and Kafka for infrastructure, and datastores such as PostgreSQL. Key Responsibilities: • Design, build and maintain backend services in a large-scale software-as-a-service platform, including distributed systems and data stores. • Take ownership of features from inception to delivery, collaborating with product, design and other engineers. • Build and maintain authentication and user lifecycle flows — signup, login, multi-factor authentication and single sign-on and user orchestration — and grow real depth in the identity domain. • Contribute to safe, incremental migration off legacy identity services using feature flags, backwards compatibility and staged rollout. • Treat security as part of the craft: handle credentials and tokens carefully, and raise concerns early rather than working around them. • Participate actively in code review, testing and the continuous improvement of our engineering practices. • Make foundational capability easy for other engineering teams to consume, treating them as the customer of platform work. • Demonstrate SafetyCulture's values — Think Customer, Open Honest Always, Better as a Team, Be Bold Bring Action. Required Skills and Experience: Technical skills: • Solid capability in back-end programming languages and frameworks, with growing expertise in building scalable systems; prior experience with Golang is highly desirable. • Practical experience owning and delivering software features end to end in a collaborative environment. • Relational data modelling and PostgreSQL — sound schema design, safe migrations, and the ability to reason about indexing and query cost. • Familiarity with cloud platforms such as AWS, including deploying, operating and debugging your own services in production. • An understanding of distributed systems concepts — application programming interface design, retries, idempotency, timeouts and eventual consistency. • Exposure to authentication, sessions or identity — login and signup flows, token handling, or standards such as single sign-on, OpenID Connect, OAuth 2.0 or SAML. Depth here is welcome but not required; appetite to go deep is. • Experience in multi-tenant systems where organisation and membership boundaries have to hold under scale and change. • A commitment to writing clean, maintainable, well-tested code, and to giving and receiving code review well. Behavioural skills: • Self-direction on well-scoped problems — takes a defined problem and drives it to done without daily direction. • Good judgement about when to push on and when to ask; raises risks and blockers early rather than burning a week. • Works effectively with product, design and engineers on other teams, and can explain a technical constraint to someone non-technical. • Builds for others, not just for self — cares whether what they ship is easy for the next team to consume. • Comfort working in a domain that is being actively re-architected around them, and with priorities that shift. Artificial intelligence skills: • Uses artificial intelligence coding assistants as a normal part of the working day, and can critically evaluate what they generate for correctness, quality and security. • Identifies practical opportunities to apply automation or intelligence within feature development. • Minimum expected: hands-on daily use with informed scepticism. Not expected at this level: defining tooling, guardrails or adoption patterns for other teams.
