Skills Required • Strong experience as an enterprise, solution, platform, or cloud architect, with a record of converting complex requirements and recurring delivery problems into practical architecture patterns. • Deep understanding of AI infrastructure across Azure, AWS, GCP, Kubernetes, containers, networking, IAM, secrets, encryption, storage, observability, CI/CD, Infrastructure as Code, resilience, and hybrid-cloud integration. • Strong knowledge of foundation models, embeddings, reasoning and multimodal models, RAG, model gateways, model routing, prompt management, structured output, context windows, tokenization, caching, model hosting, and inference trade-offs. • Hands-on understanding of evaluation frameworks and methods for models, RAG systems, and agents, including benchmark design, golden datasets, LLM-as-a-judge, rubric scoring, human review, regression testing, A/B testing, robustness, safety, latency, reliability, and cost evaluation. • Strong understanding of agent runtimes and orchestration frameworks, including planning, memory, state, tool use, MCP, A2A, single-agent and multi-agent patterns, managed CSP agent services, and open-source frameworks such as LangGraph, Semantic Kernel, AutoGen, CrewAI, or equivalent. • Knowledge of AI observability and evaluation platforms such as OpenTelemetry, MLflow, Promptflow, LangSmith, Langfuse, Arize Phoenix, Galileo, Ragas, DeepEval, TruLens, or equivalent. • Ability to evaluate platform and vendor choices objectively and document trade-offs across capability, control, portability, integration effort, operational complexity, performance, and cost. • Excellent facilitation and executive communication skills, with the ability to run architecture forums, resolve ambiguity, manage conflicting viewpoints, and build consensus without losing technical rigor. • Exceptional architecture-writing skills and attention to documentation quality, structure, traceability, diagrams, decision records, constraints, and implementation details. • Good understanding of AI security and governance, including prompt injection, excessive agency, data leakage, tool authorization, delegated identity, content controls, auditability, red teaming, model risk, and responsible AI. • Ability to work across globally distributed teams and influence senior engineers, architects, product leaders, risk partners, and vendors without relying on direct authority. Desired • 8+ years of technology experience, including significant architecture responsibility and meaningful production experience with cloud and AI platforms. • Experience establishing an architecture pattern library, architecture governance process, technology standards, or reusable reference-architecture program in a regulated enterprise. • Experience with financial-services controls, architecture governance, security design, model governance, data entitlements, audit evidence, and production-readiness processes. • Experience designing global control-plane and regional data-plane architectures, multi-region resilience, and multi-cloud or hybrid-cloud AI platforms. • Experience with enterprise data platforms, vector databases, search, Kafka, Redis, Snowflake, Databricks, API management, and retrieval or context-layer architectures. • Understanding of AI FinOps, including token and GPU consumption, model-cost allocation, routing, quotas, caching, forecasting, showback or chargeback, and optimization. • Experience prototyping or reviewing code in Python, Java, or Golang and infrastructure definitions in Terraform, Helm, or equivalent technologies. • Relevant cloud, architecture, Kubernetes, security, data, or machine-learning certifications. Key Architecture Deliverables • AI architecture principles, taxonomy, and decision framework. • Reusable reference architectures and approved patterns for model access, RAG, agents, tool integration, evaluation, and observability. • Current-state assessments, anti-pattern catalog, gap analysis, and target-state roadmaps. • Architecture Decision Records, option assessments, decision matrices, and documented trade-offs. • Pattern implementation guides with diagrams, controls, constraints, testing expectations, operational responsibilities, and example deployments. • Pattern governance backlog, lifecycle register, adoption metrics, exception records, and periodic refresh recommendations. What Success Looks Like • Teams can select an appropriate AI architecture quickly using clear decision guidance instead of creating isolated, one-off solutions. • Recurring infrastructure, connectivity, identity, data-access, evaluation, observability, agent-runtime, and governance problems are converted into reusable patterns. • Architecture forums produce clear decisions, documented rationale, accountable actions, and alignment across stakeholders with different priorities. • AI solutions consistently meet enterprise expectations for security, risk, resilience, performance, auditability, operability, and cost. • Patterns are actively adopted, implementation exceptions decline, and lessons from delivery and production operations continuously improve the architecture library.
