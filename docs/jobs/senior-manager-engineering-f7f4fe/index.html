The weaponization of AI is driving a paradigm shift in the cyber security industry from proactive to preemptive security: clients must now invest more intelligence and effort in understanding their vulnerabilities before their adversaries do (Open AI). The only way to achieve this is to simulate future attack scenarios at scale, and to run them in high fidelity digital replicas of production environments (Gartner/Forrester). SimSpace serves as the AI Proving Grounds to generate hypersynthetic data which pressure tests controls, tools, teams, and agentic capabilities in full kill chain attacks hidden in realistic environmental traffic and patterns of life. Founded in 2015 by experts from U.S. Cyber Command and MIT Lincoln Laboratory, the platform was initially conceived to assist in Offensive Cyber Operations (OCO), to quickly replicate target environments to conduct mission rehearsal of human operators and their tools to deny (disrupt, degrade, destroy) or deceive (manipulate or exfiltrate data) adversaries. The company has since expanded to serve over a dozen allied governments in OCO/DCO as well as critical infrastructure enterprises around the world. The company is actively investing in the Americas, Asia Pacific, Europe and the Middle East through direct sales and channel partners. SimSpace has launched the AI Proving Grounds Consortium, leading the revolution by creating an open platform for the InfoSec ecosystem to train, validate and operationalize their agentic capabilities before committing to production. Why join SimSpace? We are a team united by a shared purpose and four core values: Serve to Protect, Acquire Understanding, Teamwork without Borders, and Operate as Innovators. We value candid, constructive feedback and create opportunities for personal, professional, and financial growth. Our vision, “Defend the Free World,” drives our mission: “Defeat Any Adversary in Any Cyber Terrain at Machine Speed.” How Do We Work? We listen to customers and colleagues, challenge assumptions, and turn ideas into meaningful results. We expect everyone to embrace emerging technologies, simplify and automate work, and use AI to learn, solve problems, and help deliver quality with speed. We apply human judgment and take responsibility for outcomes. Whether remote or office-based, we collaborate across teams and time zones. We invite you to apply today! About the Role SimSpace’s AI Proving Grounds (AIPG) gives customers a place to evaluate AI agents in cyber ranges. The Attack and User Emulation team builds fullstack workflows for attack and user emulation, with an emphasis on backend engineering. Customers can configure, observe and repeat those workflows across applications and execution environments. You’ll build and grow the team and report to the Director of Environmental Realism. You’re accountable for the team’s health, the quality of what it ships and the careers of its engineers. You’ll lead through the team and stay close enough to the work to review designs, contribute focused changes and unblock difficult problems. The team owns both attack emulation and user emulation. Scenario Development provides adversary content, and range and platform teams own their shared services. You’ll lead work that spans those teams and agree on responsibilities and capacity before committing to delivery. What You'll Do Lead and Grow Your Team • Build a team of fullstack engineers with strong backend skills. Agree on hiring priorities, onboarding and interim technical coverage with the Director. • Own regular one-on-ones, performance feedback and career development. Coach engineers through decisions and give them room to take ownership of features. • Set the team’s goals and priorities. Agree on initial scope and timing with the Director and Product based on staffing, support needs and partner commitments. • Partner with the Staff Engineer, who leads design and integration across features. Stay involved in architecture and code reviews so you can coach the team and help it make difficult trade-offs. Make Spec-Driven, AI-Native Delivery the Norm • Make spec-driven development standard practice. Have the team write specs, plans and acceptance criteria before AI coding agents implement changes, then verify the work against those criteria. • Make AI-native workflows the team’s default. Judge whether they’re helping by looking at delivery, quality and predictability. • Set standards for who specifies, reviews, tests and owns AI-generated work. Require engineers to verify changes before release. Unblock the Team and Drive Delivery • Own delivery from configuration through execution and verified results, including diagnosis and recovery when a workflow fails. Agree on changes to shared components with the teams that own them. • Define the workflow with Product and partners. Agree on scope, interfaces, acceptance criteria and who will use the result before the team commits to delivery. • Remove technical and cross-team blockers before they delay delivery. Write focused production changes, tests or automation and help diagnose failures across service boundaries. • Define what counts as a successful emulation run and what evidence the team needs to verify it. Lead integration testing for identities, compatibility, intended effects and cleanup after failures. Make workflows repeatable with tested automation, useful diagnostics and clear release and support responsibilities. Lead Initiatives and Drive Engineering Maturity • Take charge of initiatives that span teams. Frame the problem, clarify ownership and partner commitments, and drive the work through delivery and operational handover. • Improve end-to-end testing in CI/CD so teams can trust it for release decisions. Share what the team learns about integration testing, spec-driven development and AI-native workflows with other teams. Communicate Up and Across • Keep your director and the leadership team informed about delivery, quality and team health. Use evidence and trends to show where the team is progressing and where it needs help. • Keep Product, QA, Scenario Development, and the range and platform teams informed about progress, risks and trade-offs. Raise risks early and bring a proposed way forward. • Partner with Product Management to turn priorities into a delivery plan. Account for support, maintenance and dependencies, and explain what needs to move when priorities or capacity change.
